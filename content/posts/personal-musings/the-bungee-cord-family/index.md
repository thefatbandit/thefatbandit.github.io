What comes to mind when I say *family*? Father, mother, brother, that one uncle who asks about your marks at every wedding.

But what if I told you there are people who shape who you are just by being around you? You don't think of them as family. You don't even notice them most days. And then you're separated from them, and suddenly you miss them the way a traveller misses an oasis once the sand starts burning his feet again. For a few years, and if you're lucky for the rest of your life, these people become your lifeline.

I'm talking about friends. Especially the ones we make in college.

## The cord

These are the people we spend the most important (and, not so surprisingly, the most depressing) years of our lives with. They become our support line. They become our **bungee cord** to life's cliffhangers, snapping us back to safety just in time. Not too early, because we need to learn to fight our own battles. Not too late, because nobody wants to go *splat*.

On a normal day, they show you no mercy. They talk over you, tease you, steal your Maggi, and laugh at your sorrows like it's a stand-up special. And honestly, that's fair. Everyone is fighting their own uphill battle; there's no point loading it onto each other's backs.

But the moment real trouble shows up, there they are. Your infallible cover. Ready to fight the world for you if it comes to that, or at the very least sit with you at 3 a.m. outside the hostel canteen saying nothing at all. And somehow that's enough. Knowing you aren't alone is what makes you get up, dust yourself off, and walk back towards whatever life just threw at you.

## Apes together strong

People like to say that boys don't share their feelings, with friends, with family, with anyone. I'd like to officially blame the Bungee Family for this.

That strong, invisible support behind you makes you feel invincible. Why talk about the problem when you *know* you have backup? There's also a quiet respect inside this family. We assume the other person can handle what's in front of them, and that they'll call if they can't. Nobody hovers. But the support is absolutely there.

They give real meaning to *"Apes together strong."* So no, we won't tell each other everything (though, contrary to popular belief, we tell each other a lot more than you'd think). But we always know exactly when another ape is in trouble. And we show up, usually with snacks and a terrible joke, which is the ape version of a hug.

## What you take home

A family you feel closer to than your own parents for four or five years of your life. The people who snap you back to reality when you drift too far. The invisible army standing behind you in every photo, every exam, every heartbreak.

The Bungee Cord Family.

Degrees get framed and forgotten in a drawer. The CGPA stops mattering the day you get your first job. But this family? This is the best thing you take home from college at graduation.

To mine: thank you for every time you let me fall, and every time you didn't.
